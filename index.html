AI

AI has been part of my work since my first year of computer science. It started with search and constraint solving as an undergraduate, continued with classic machine learning in production at Salesforce and a master's degree centred on deep reinforcement learning, and today it means bringing large language models and agents into a production platform.

2011 – 2012

Classical AI at the Hebrew University

B.Sc. · Introduction to Artificial Intelligence and a guided project with Prof. Jeff Rosenschein

Back then the field was called classical AI and machine learning: search, planning, constraint satisfaction and the beginnings of statistical learning. Two projects from that year, both with Ilia Pogrebezky, have stayed with me.

  • Wedding planner. Guest seating as a constraint satisfaction problem with a cost function: tables of different sizes, must-sit-with and must-not-sit-with rules, hard and soft constraints. We compared backtracking against local search methods such as hill climbing with random restarts, simulated annealing and tabu search, and shipped a working system.
  • Peg solitaire solver. A game AI for the diamond peg board built on uninformed and informed search: BFS, DFS, iterative deepening, A*, IDA* and weighted A* with a set of hand-designed heuristics, benchmarked against each other.

2018 – 2019

Deep reinforcement learning and DQN

M.Sc. final paper and project · The Open University of Israel · supervised by Dr. Mireille Avigal

My master's degree focused on reinforcement learning and, above all, on Deep Q-Networks. The final paper, Deep Reinforcement Learning: Theory, Applications and a Look to the Future, builds the theory up from Markov decision processes, dynamic programming, Monte Carlo and temporal-difference methods to DQN and its successors, then surveys where the field was heading. The accompanying project is a DQN agent that learns to play Pac-Man from raw pixels: convolutional network, experience replay, frame stacking and a target network, written from scratch in Python.

Now

Large language models and agents at FundGuard

Software Architect & AI Lead

I lead FundGuard's AI work. The first phase was making existing features LLM-ready: data mapping, reconciliation and exception handling that can propose, explain and, with enough confidence, act. The current phase is building agents into the product itself, so that fund accountants and operations teams can run an investment accounting platform in plain language, and so that customers' own agents can work against governed, real-time accounting data.

The architectural principles behind this are public: a single real-time system of record, complete auditability for every decision an agent makes, and open interoperability through the Model Context Protocol and agent-to-agent standards.
